# Masterbrand identity — candidates as test stimuli

> **Status: nothing here is a decision.** The decision brief is explicit: do not lock the
> final masterbrand name, logo, exact palette or font system before parent language testing.
> These six candidates exist so you have something concrete to put in front of parents in the
> Sept 7–18 interviews. Treat them as stimuli, not as a shortlist you have already narrowed.
>
> The repo currently uses **Marsam / مرسم** as a working placeholder. Change it in one command
> once the sprint tells you what to call it: `./business/rename-brand.sh "Name" "الاسم"`.

## What the name has to carry

From the brand architecture in the brief, the masterbrand owns the long-term promise —
*curious, capable children learn by making* — and must survive the product line growing past
coloring into sticker stories, activity kits, postcards and educator packs.

That gives four hard constraints:

1. **Arabic-first and genuinely bilingual.** The name has to be born in Arabic and read
   naturally in Latin script, not an English name with Arabic bolted on as decoration.
2. **Expandable.** A name that means "coloring" caps the brand at the flagship.
3. **Not touristy, not ceremonial, not babyish.** The brief bans all three.
4. **Ownable.** A masterbrand needs a registrable mark, a domain and clean handles.
   Common dictionary words are warm but weak here.

### If forced to rank today

`Naqsh` and `Kashta` are the two that are both distinctive enough to own and rooted enough to
do cultural work. `Marsam` is the safest to *say* and the riskiest to *register*. `Lawwin` is
the strongest flagship name and the weakest masterbrand.

Do not act on that ranking. It is a hypothesis written before a single parent has been asked.

## How to test names without leading the answer

Names are the easiest thing in a research session to get a false positive on, because parents
will be polite about a name and honest about a product. Keep the name test **last**, after the
prototype has been shown and priced, and run it like this:

- Present all six on one card, Arabic-first, with no logo, no color, no explanation.
- Ask what kind of company each one sounds like — **before** saying what yours does.
- Then ask which one they would expect to sell a premium children's activity product, and why.
- Then ask the disqualifying question: *is there any of these you would not buy from, or that
  sounds off to you?* Negative reactions are far more diagnostic than preferences here.
- Record the exact words parents use to describe the good ones. Those words are worth more
  than the vote, and they feed the copy in `04-launch/`.

## Before any money goes into identity

- [ ] **SAIP trademark search** (Saudi Authority for Intellectual Property) in the relevant
      classes — printed matter and educational goods (Nice class 16) at minimum, and consider
      class 28 if bundling toys/pencils, class 41 if educator packs become a service.
- [ ] Domain plus Instagram / Snapchat / TikTok handle availability as one bundle. A name with
      no matching handles costs more later than picking the second-favourite now.
- [ ] Arabic reading check with a native editorial reviewer — including how it reads to a child.
- [ ] Confirm the name carries no unintended regional or dialect meaning outside your own.

## Working visual territory — unapproved

Carried over from the brief, unchanged, to keep the design conversation anchored:
contemporary Saudi warmth; tactile paper craft; bright but calm color; child-made marks;
accurate motifs abstracted with care. Saudi green can anchor the system but should not
dominate every page.

Two additions the brief implies but does not state:

- **The palette must survive being colored on.** Line art is the product. Whatever brand color
  you choose has to sit next to a child's crayon work without fighting it — which argues for a
  restrained, high-contrast identity and against a saturated brand color on every spread.
- **Do not mimic official symbols.** The brief bans it, and it is also the fastest route to a
  regulatory problem. Keep flags, emblems and official marks out of the product entirely.
