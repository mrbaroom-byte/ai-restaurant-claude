# Claim discipline — what you may and may not say

> Applies to every surface: product pages, packaging, ads, creator briefs, customer support
> replies, and anything a founder says on camera. One unsupported developmental claim can cost
> you the trust the whole positioning is built on — and in a category aimed at children,
> it can cost you more than trust.

## The rule

The brief sets it plainly: use **"supports," "encourages," "designed for."** Do not claim
measurable learning outcomes until the product has been evaluated appropriately.

The distinction that matters: you may describe **what the product is and what the child does**.
You may not assert **what happens to the child as a result**.

## Approved / banned language

| Say this | Not this | Why |
|----------|----------|-----|
| Designed to support fine-motor practice | Improves fine-motor skills | The second is a measured outcome you have not measured |
| Encourages observation and description | Builds observation skills | Same |
| Bilingual prompts, Arabic-first | Teaches Arabic / improves literacy | You are not a curriculum and have not been evaluated as one |
| Screen-light activity | Reduces screen addiction / fixes screen time | Clinical framing; also the brief bans anti-device messaging |
| Ages 5–8 | Suitable for all ages 4–9 | The brief flags the too-broad age claim as a top-five risk |
| Twenty single-sided tear-out sheets, 140–160 gsm | Premium / luxury / museum-quality | Specifics build trust; adjectives are what every competitor already says |
| Reviewed by a named Arabic editorial and cultural reviewer | Culturally authentic / approved | Name the process, not a verdict about yourself |
| Inspired by the places, nature and stories of Saudi Arabia | Official / endorsed / national | Never imply official status or endorsement |

## Hard bans

- **No official-symbol mimicry.** No flag, no state emblem, no Vision 2030 logo, no ministry
  or authority marks, no visual arrangement that could read as government endorsement.
- **No named-institution association** you do not have in writing — no school, ministry,
  museum or authority mentioned as a partner, user or approver.
- **No measurable outcome claims** — no IQ, reading level, school-readiness, developmental
  milestone, attention span or screen-time reduction figure.
- **No health or therapeutic claims** — nothing about calm, anxiety, focus as a treatment,
  sensory therapy or special-needs efficacy.
- **No comparative claim against a named competitor** without evidence you would be willing to
  show a regulator.
- **No fake scarcity or fake social proof** — no invented review counts, no "1,000 sold" before
  you have sold 1,000, no stock counters that do not reflect stock.

## Reviews and paid content

The brief calls for separating paid content from organic reviews. In practice:

- Every gifted, paid or commissioned post must be labelled as an ad by the creator, in Arabic,
  in the post itself — not only in a bio or a comment. Brief creators on this in writing and
  keep the written brief.
- Never publish a review you solicited with an incentive without disclosing the incentive.
- Verified-photo reviews only on the product page. If you cannot verify the purchase, do not
  present it as a customer review.
- Do not edit a review's wording. You may translate it if you label it as translated.

## The correction protocol

The brief requires a "correction promise" as trust proof on the product page. That promise has
to be real and pre-agreed, or it is worse than not making it:

1. **Intake.** A single monitored channel for accuracy and representation concerns, published
   on the product page and on pack.
2. **Acknowledge within 2 business days**, in the language the concern was raised in.
3. **Route to the independent cultural/editorial reviewer** — not to marketing — for a factual
   finding.
4. **If a sheet is wrong**: stop selling that print run's affected stock, publish the
   correction, and reprint the sheet. Offer a replacement sheet free to every buyer of the
   affected run, shipped at your cost.
5. **Log every case** with date, concern, finding and action, in one file. That log is your
   evidence of good faith if the same question is asked publicly later.

Budget for this. `03-economics/` carries a corrections reserve for exactly this reason —
a reprint of one sheet across a pilot run is a survivable cost, and only if it is planned.

## Pre-publication sign-off

Nothing consumer-facing ships without both boxes ticked:

- [ ] **Cultural/editorial review** — accuracy of every fact, name, place, spelling, diacritic
      and depiction; tone check against "warm, curious, contemporary, respectful, child-capable."
- [ ] **Claims review** — every sentence checked against the table above by someone who did not
      write the copy.

Keep both sign-offs as dated files. `05-operations/compliance-checklist.md` covers the
statutory side — legal deposit, VAT, e-commerce disclosure — which is separate from this and
also mandatory.
