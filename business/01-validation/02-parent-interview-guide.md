# Parent depth interview — 30 minutes

12 sessions. Riyadh (6) and Jeddah (6). Run in the parent's preferred language; the Arabic
below is the version to say out loud when they choose Arabic, not a translation to read from.

**You need:** one unbranded prototype pad, a printed sheet at final paper weight, a phone timer,
this guide, and the language-bank sheet from `analysis/sprint-workbook.xlsx`.

**You must not bring:** the brand name, a logo, a price, a pitch, or the word "educational."

---

## The discipline

The brief says it once and it governs everything here: **test the benefit first; let culture
provide the reason to believe.** In an interview that translates into three rules.

1. **Ask about the past, not the future.** "Would you buy this?" is worthless — it measures
   politeness. "What was the last one you bought, and what happened after it arrived?" is
   evidence. Behaviour that already happened is the only reliable data a parent has.
2. **Show before you explain, always.** The reveal in Section 4 is the highest-value 90 seconds
   of the sprint, and you get exactly one per parent. Spend it before you have contaminated it.
3. **Chase the flat answers.** "It's nice" means you have learned nothing yet. Every "nice,"
   "good," "cute" gets one follow-up: *what makes it that?* Then stop — twice is interrogation.

Silence is a tool. After a parent finishes an answer, wait three full seconds before speaking.
The second half of the answer, which is usually the honest half, arrives in that gap.

## 0. Opening — 2 min

> "Thank you for making time. I'm working on a physical activity product for children and I'm
> at the stage of trying to understand how parents actually choose these things. There are no
> right answers, and I'm not selling anything today — if you tell me something isn't good, that
> is genuinely the most useful thing that can happen in this conversation. May I record audio
> just so I don't have to take notes while you talk? I'll delete it after I write it up."

> «شكرًا لوقتك. أنا أعمل على منتج نشاط ملموس للأطفال، وأحاول أفهم كيف يختار الأهالي هذه
> الأشياء فعليًا. ما فيه إجابات صح وغلط، وأنا ما أبيع لك شيئًا اليوم — إذا قلت لي إن شيئًا
> ما ليس جيدًا، فهذا أنفع شيء ممكن يصير في هذه المحادثة. تسمح لي أسجل صوت فقط عشان ما أنشغل
> بالكتابة؟ راح أحذفه بعد ما أكتب الملاحظات.»

Note the refusal if they say no. Do not record. Take notes instead.

## 1. The last purchase — 6 min

*Purpose: real behaviour, real occasions, real disappointment. This is the section that
produces the language bank.*

1. Tell me about the last non-screen activity or toy you bought for your child. **What happened
   after it arrived?**
   حدثني عن آخر نشاط أو لعبة غير إلكترونية اشتريتها لطفلك. **وش صار بعد ما وصلت؟**

2. Where did you buy it, and what made you pick that one over the others you saw?
   من وين اشتريتها، ووش خلاك تختارها من بين الباقي؟

3. Did your child actually use it? How many times? *(If it stopped — what happened?)*
   هل استخدمها طفلك فعلًا؟ كم مرة؟ *(وإذا توقف — وش صار؟)*

4. Was there anything about it that disappointed you when you saw it in person?
   هل فيه شيء خيّب ظنك لما شفتها على الطبيعة؟

5. When did you buy it — was there an occasion, or was it an ordinary day?
   متى اشتريتها — كان فيه مناسبة، ولا يوم عادي؟

> **Listen for:** the occasion (gift / travel / weekend / "he was bored"), the deciding factor,
> and the disappointment. Q4 is where you find out what "quality proof" has to defeat.

---

## 2. The screen tension — 5 min

*Purpose: test the "intentional screen-balancer" hypothesis without planting it.*

6. Walk me through a normal weekday afternoon at home. What is your child doing?
   خذني في عصر يوم عادي في البيت. وش يسوي طفلك؟

7. Is there anything you'd like to change about that? *(Do not say "screens.")*
   فيه شيء تتمنى تغيره في هذا الوقت؟

8. *(Only if they raise screens themselves)* Tell me more about that.
   *(فقط إذا هم ذكروا الشاشات)* حدثني أكثر عن هذي النقطة.

9. When your child says "I'm bored," what actually happens next?
   لما يقول طفلك «أنا ملليت»، وش يصير بعدها فعليًا؟

> **The trap:** if you introduce the word "screens," every parent will perform concern about
> screens, because they know that is the socially correct answer. The finding you need is
> whether it comes up **unprompted** — and in how many of the 12. Record that count. It is the
> single cleanest test of the primary-buyer hypothesis in the entire sprint.

---

## 3. What makes an activity worth paying for — 5 min

10. When does a coloring or activity product feel *educational enough* to be worth paying for?
    متى يحس المرء إن منتج التلوين أو النشاط «تعليمي كفاية» ويستاهل الدفع؟

11. And when does one feel like it's just filler?
    ومتى يحس إنه مجرد تعبئة وقت؟

12. What makes Saudi-themed children's products feel authentic — or clichéd, or too ceremonial?
    وش يخلي منتجات الأطفال ذات الطابع السعودي تحس إنها أصيلة — أو مبتذلة، أو رسمية زيادة؟

> **Listen for:** the exact phrases parents use to draw the line between "useful" and "filler."
> That boundary is where the price sits, and their words go straight onto the product page.
> Q12 is your cliché map — write down every specific thing named as tired.

---

## 4. The reveal — 7 min

**Hand over the prototype. Say nothing. Start the timer. Wait.**

Let them open it, turn pages, and talk. Do not narrate. Do not correct. Do not answer questions
with information yet — reflect them back ("what would you expect?").

13. *(After they have looked)* What do you think this is, and who is it for?
    وش تتوقع هذا الشيء، ولمن؟

14. What would you expect to pay for it?
    كم تتوقع سعره؟

15. What would your child do with it — alone, with you, or as a gift? What would stop them?
    وش راح يسوي طفلك فيه — لحاله، معك، أو كهدية؟ ووش ممكن يمنعه؟

16. Is there anything here that feels wrong, missing, or not quite right to you?
    فيه شيء هنا يحس غلط، أو ناقص، أو ما ضبط؟

17. *(Point at one discovery prompt)* What do you make of this bit?
    *(أشر إلى أحد أسئلة الاكتشاف)* وش رأيك في هذا الجزء؟

> **Record, verbatim and in order:** the first three words out of their mouth; which page they
> turn to first; whether they read a discovery prompt aloud unprompted; the number they say in
> Q14 **before** any anchor from you; and every hesitation.
>
> **The number in Q14 is only clean once.** If you have said a price, or reacted to their guess,
> the answer is contaminated for that parent and for the price analysis. Say nothing after they
> answer except "thank you" — no "interesting," no eyebrows.

---

## 5. Buying it online — 4 min

18. Imagine you saw this online from a brand you've never heard of. What would you need to see
    before you'd buy it?
    تخيل إنك شفته على الإنترنت من علامة ما تعرفها. وش تحتاج تشوف قبل ما تشتري؟

19. What would make you close the page immediately?
    وش يخليك تسكر الصفحة على طول؟

20. Where would you expect to come across something like this?
    وين تتوقع تصادف شيئًا مثل هذا؟

> **Listen for:** the proof list (photos, video, paper, size, reviews, returns, who is behind
> it) and the disqualifiers. This section writes `04-launch/product-page-copy.md`. Q19 is
> usually more actionable than Q18.

---

## 6. Names — 1 min, last, optional

Only if there is time, and only now. Show the six candidates from
`00-strategy/brand-identity-options.md` on one card, Arabic-first, no logo, no color.

21. What kind of company does each of these sound like?
22. Any of these you'd *not* buy from, or that sounds off?

Record the negatives. Ignore the preferences.

---

## Close — 1 min

> "Last thing — is there anything I should have asked you about and didn't?"
> «آخر شيء — فيه شيء كان المفروض أسألك عنه وما سألت؟»

Then pay the incentive, thank them, and stop talking about the product.

---

## Same-day debrief — 15 minutes, non-negotiable

Within an hour, fill one row per parent in the workbook's **Language Bank** tab:

| Field | Note |
|-------|------|
| Verbatim: what they said it was (Q13) | Their words, not your summary |
| Verbatim: the "useful vs filler" line (Q10/11) | The phrase that draws the boundary |
| Price guess (Q14) | The number, unanchored |
| Screens raised unprompted? | Y/N — this is a counted variable |
| Top objection | The thing that would actually stop the purchase |
| Proof demanded (Q18) | List |
| Deal-breaker (Q19) | List |
| Best single sentence | The one line good enough to become copy |
| Your confidence they'd buy | 1–5, your read, recorded before you know the others |

After all 12, count: how many raised screens unprompted; how many correctly identified the
product without explanation; the median unanchored price guess; and the objections that
appeared **three or more times**. Those four numbers are what the gate review argues about.
