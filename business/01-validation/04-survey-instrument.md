# Quantitative survey — 150–250 qualified parents

Mobile-first. Arabic-first, English available. Target completion under 6 minutes.
Field Sept 19, close when n ≥ 150 qualified (target 250).

**What this survey is for:** ranking the four value drivers against each other, and sizing the
objections found in the depth interviews. **What it is not for:** setting the price. Price is
decided by the paid test and the contribution model, not by asking people what they would pay.

---

## Screener (repeat — never trust an earlier qualification)

S1. Do you live in Saudi Arabia? → No: terminate
S2. Do you have a child aged 4–9? → No: terminate
S3. Have you bought a physical children's product online in the last 6 months? → No: terminate
S4. Do you or a household member work in marketing/research/publishing/children's products? → Yes: terminate

Record city, child's age, parent role, home language, nationality.

---

## Section A — Behaviour (establishes the base rate)

**A1.** In the last 6 months, how many physical activity products (coloring, craft, sticker,
puzzle, activity books) have you bought for your child?
`0 / 1 / 2–3 / 4–5 / 6+`

**A2.** Thinking about the last one — how many times did your child actually use it?
`Never / Once / 2–5 times / More than 5 times / Still using it`

**A3.** How many children's books are in your home right now?
`None / 1–2 / 3–5 / 6–10 / More than 10`

> A3 is the one question that tests the brief's headline demand evidence against your own
> buyer. GASTAT reports 59.8% of under-fives have no children's books at home and only 9.2%
> have three or more. If your qualified, online-buying sample looks nothing like that — and it
> probably won't — then the "white space" argument describes the population, not your customer.
> That distinction changes who you target and how big you think this is. Report it either way.

**A4.** On a normal weekday, roughly how long does your child spend on a screen for fun?
`Under 1 hour / 1–2 hours / 2–3 hours / More than 3 hours / Not sure`

**A5.** Have you bought a children's product with Saudi themes or content before?
`Yes / No / Not sure`

**A6.** *(If A5 = Yes)* How did it turn out? — open text, one line

---

## Section B — Forced trade-offs (the core of the survey)

> Rating scales will tell you that parents want all four things very much, which you already
> know and cannot act on. Only forced choice produces a ranking you can put on a product page.
> Both mechanics below are included on purpose: B1–B4 give a robust aggregate ranking,
> B5 gives an intuitive one, and agreement between them is your confidence check.

### B1–B4. Best/worst choice sets

Show each respondent **four** sets. In each set, five attributes; pick the **most** and the
**least** important. Rotate which five appear, and rotate their order.

Attribute pool:

| Code | Attribute (EN) | Attribute (AR) |
|------|----------------|----------------|
| LEARN | It has clear learning value | فيه قيمة تعليمية واضحة |
| SCREEN | It keeps my child busy without a screen | يشغل طفلي بدون شاشة |
| CULTURE | The content reflects Saudi Arabia | المحتوى يعكس السعودية |
| GIFT | It looks good enough to give as a gift | شكله يليق كهدية |
| ALONE | My child can use it without my help | طفلي يقدر يستخدمه بدون مساعدتي |
| QUALITY | The paper and printing feel premium | الورق والطباعة تحس فاخرة |
| ARABIC | The Arabic comes first, not as a translation | العربية أولًا، مو ترجمة |
| PRICE | The price is low | السعر منخفض |

**Set 1:** LEARN, SCREEN, CULTURE, GIFT, PRICE
**Set 2:** LEARN, ALONE, QUALITY, ARABIC, PRICE
**Set 3:** SCREEN, CULTURE, QUALITY, GIFT, ALONE
**Set 4:** LEARN, SCREEN, ARABIC, GIFT, QUALITY

Question wording:

> Of these five, which is **most** important to you when buying an activity product for your
> child? And which is **least** important?
> من بين هذه الخمسة، أيها **الأهم** لك عند شراء منتج نشاط لطفلك؟ وأيها **الأقل** أهمية؟

*Scoring:* count(most) − count(least) per attribute, divided by the number of sets it appeared
in. Range −1 to +1. The workbook does this automatically.

**PRICE is in the pool deliberately.** It is the calibration anchor: if "the price is low" beats
LEARN and SCREEN, you do not have a premium proposition and no amount of message testing will
create one.

### B5. Constant sum

> You have 100 points. Give more points to what matters more. Total must equal 100.
> عندك ١٠٠ نقطة. أعطِ نقاطًا أكثر لما يهمك أكثر. المجموع لازم يساوي ١٠٠.

`Learning value ___ | Keeps them off a screen ___ | Saudi content ___ | Giftable quality ___ | Low price ___`

## Section C — Proposition test

Show **one** proposition per respondent, randomized, evenly split. Identical product image,
identical layout, only the words change. This mirrors the three message cells in
`04-launch/` so the survey and the paid test can be read against each other.

**Cell A — creative-learning lead**
> Creative time that teaches. Twenty screen-light Saudi-inspired sheets for children ages 5–8.
> وقت إبداعي يعلّم. عشرون ورقة بعيدة عن الشاشة، مستوحاة من السعودية، لأعمار ٥–٨.

**Cell B — culture lead**
> Discover Saudi Arabia, one color at a time. A bilingual creative journey through places,
> nature and traditions.
> اكتشف السعودية، لونًا بلون. رحلة إبداعية ثنائية اللغة بين الأماكن والطبيعة والعادات.

**Cell C — screen balance**
> A better answer to "I'm bored." Open the pad. Choose a page. Create without another screen.
> إجابة أفضل لـ«أنا ملليت». افتح الدفتر. اختر صفحة. أبدع بدون شاشة ثانية.

**C1.** How well does this describe something you'd want for your child? `1–5`
**C2.** How different is this from what you can already buy? `1–5`
**C3.** In your own words, what would your child get out of this? — open text
**C4.** What's the first question you'd want answered before buying? — open text

> **C1 is the relevance gate.** The threshold is ≥70% rating the *winning* proposition 4 or 5.
> **C3 is the most valuable question in the survey** — it tells you what the message actually
> communicated, as opposed to what you intended. If C3 answers come back as "coloring," your
> learning lead is not landing regardless of what C1 scores.

## Section D — Objections

**D1.** How much would each of these worry you when buying online from a brand you don't know?
`1 = not at all, 5 = a lot`

| | |
|---|---|
| The quality won't match the photos | الجودة ما تطابق الصور |
| It's too easy or too hard for my child | صعب أو سهل زيادة على طفلي |
| My child will use it once and stop | طفلي يستخدمه مرة ويتركه |
| The Saudi content will feel clichéd | المحتوى السعودي يحس مبتذل |
| The Arabic will be poor or an afterthought | العربية ضعيفة أو مضافة لاحقًا |
| Delivery or returns will be a problem | التوصيل أو الإرجاع فيه مشكلة |
| The price is too high for what it is | السعر مرتفع مقابل ما فيه |

**D2.** Which single one of those would most likely stop you? — single choice

**D3.** What would you need to see on the page to get past it? — open text

## Section E — Occasion and price context

**E1.** Would you buy this mainly for your own child, as a gift, or both?
**E2.** When would you most likely buy it? `Ordinary week / School holiday / Travel / Birthday gift / Ramadan or Eid / National Day or Founding Day / Other`

> E2 is the evergreen-vs-event test. The brief's number-one risk is "patriotism without repeat
> use." If National Day and Founding Day dominate E2, your revenue is seasonal, your inventory
> plan is wrong, and the masterbrand positioning needs defending rather than assuming.

**E3.** For a 20-sheet premium activity pad like this, what would feel like a fair price?
`Under 25 / 25–34 / 35–44 / 45–54 / 55–69 / 70+ SAR`

**E4.** At what price would it feel too expensive to consider? `open numeric`
**E5.** At what price would you start to doubt the quality? `open numeric`

> E3–E5 are a light Van Westendorp. Treat the output as **context for the paid test's price
> range, not as the price.** Stated willingness to pay reliably overstates at the low end and
> understates at the premium end. The decision rule stays contribution per qualified visitor.

---

## Close

**E6.** If this existed today, would you want to hear when it launches? `Yes → email/phone / No`

> The opt-in rate here is a real intent signal and costs one question. Treat these contacts
> under PDPL: separate consent, stated purpose, and a working unsubscribe from the first message.

## Analysis plan — write it down before the data arrives

Pre-committing the analysis is what stops a disappointing result from being reinterpreted into
a good one at the gate review.

| Output | Method | Feeds |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Attribute ranking | Best-worst scores; cross-check against B5 constant sum | Message hierarchy, PDP proof bar |
| Winning proposition | C1 top-2-box by cell, tested for significance | Which landing page gets the budget |
| Relevance gate | ≥70% at 4–5 on the winner | Gate 1 in `05-decision-gates.md` |
| Comprehension | C3 open-text coded into themes | Whether the message survives contact |
| Objection sizing | D1 means, D2 frequency | PDP trust blocks, FAQ order |
| Occasion mix | E2 distribution | Evergreen vs seasonal risk, inventory |
| Price context | E3 mode; E4/E5 crossover | The range for the paid price test only |
| Subgroups | By city, child age, home language, Saudi/resident | Targeting; and whether 5–8 holds |

**Minimum n for any subgroup claim: 30.** Below that, report it as a hypothesis and say so out
loud at the gate review. With n=150 across three cells you have ~50 per cell — enough to see a
large difference, not enough to resolve a small one. If the cells land within a few points of
each other, the honest finding is *"message did not differentiate"*, and the decision passes to
the paid test where real money reveals real intent.
