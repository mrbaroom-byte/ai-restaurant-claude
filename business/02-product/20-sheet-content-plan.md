# Colors of Saudi Arabia — ألوان السعودية
## Editorial specification, all 20 sheets

> **Every fact on this page is a draft awaiting the independent cultural and editorial review
> budgeted at SAR 2,500 (`03-economics/`).** Nothing here goes to print unverified. Facts written
> for a 5–8 year old have to be *both* simple and true, and that is exactly the combination that
> produces confident errors. The reviewer's job is to check each one against a primary source and
> sign the sheet off by number.

> **Machine-readable copy:** `sheets.json` holds this same data and is the single source of
> truth read by the print prototype and the landing pages, so the site and the product cannot
> drift apart. Edit `sheets.json`, then re-run both builds. This document holds the rationale.

---

## Design system across all 20 sheets

| Element | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| Format | A4 (210 × 297 mm), single-sided, tear-out |
| Line art | Black line only, no greyscale fills, no fine hatching. Minimum stroke 1.2 pt at print size |
| Safe margin | 12 mm from every trimmed edge — nothing colorable inside it |
| Difficulty icon | 1, 2 or 3 filled circles, top-right, consistent position on every sheet |
| Sheet number | Bottom-left, Arabic-Indic and Latin numerals |
| Discovery prompt | Bottom band, Arabic first (right), English beneath (left). Max 18 Arabic words |
| Creative prompt | Inside the illustration area, in a hand-lettered style distinct from the fact text |
| Talk-together prompt | 7 sheets only, small, bottom-right, marked with a consistent icon |
| Reverse | Blank. No printing on the back — it is the display side once the sheet is torn out |

**Why single-sided is a feature, not a saving:** the reverse is what a parent sticks on a fridge
or a wall. Say that on the product page — it converts a cost decision into a benefit.

### Content mix

| Pillar | Sheets | Count |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Nature and land | 1–7 | 7 |
| Architecture and heritage places | 8–11 | 4 |
| Craft and making | 12–14 | 3 |
| Everyday life | 15–17 | 3 |
| Contemporary and future | 18–19 | 2 |
| Open finale | 20 | 1 |

Deliberately: **no flags, no state emblems, no official symbols, and no single-region
dominance.** The brief bans symbol mimicry and warns against monuments-only cliché. Regions
covered: Najd, Hijaz, Asir, the Eastern Province, Tabuk/AlUla, Jazan and Taif.

### Difficulty curve

Sheets are numbered in a deliberate order — 1 and 2 are the easiest, difficulty rises to the
middle, and 20 is wide open. Children will not use them in order. That is fine: the order exists
so that a parent flipping the pad in a shop, or a child taking the top sheet, meets an easy win
first.

## The completion certificate

Not one of the 20. A separate card, same paper stock, bound at the back.

*AR:* شهادة إتمام — أنجز/أنجزت رحلته الإبداعية في ألوان السعودية
*EN:* Certificate of Completion — completed their creative journey through Colors of Saudi Arabia

With a space for the child's name, the date, and a line for a parent's signature.

Optional sticker seal in the gift bundle only. **If a sticker or pencils are added to any SKU,
check SASO/SABER toy-safety obligations before importing or bundling** — see
`05-operations/compliance-checklist.md`. This is the most commonly missed requirement in this
product category and it is easier to design around than to fix at customs.

## Talk-together placement — 7 of 20

Sheets **2, 6, 9, 13, 15, 16, 19**.

Spread across the pad so no run of four sheets is without one, but never on consecutive sheets.
The brief's requirement is that these support co-play *without requiring a parent every time* —
so they are small, optional, visually secondary to the creative prompt, and never phrased as an
instruction the child must complete.

## Editorial rules the illustrator must be briefed on

1. **Contemporary, not costume.** People wear what people wear now. Historic dress only where
   the sheet is explicitly historical.
2. **Children of visibly different backgrounds appear across the pad.** The brand is open to
   every family living in the Kingdom; the artwork has to be too.
3. **No faces on religious subjects, no religious sites as coloring subjects.** Not in scope
   for this product, and not a judgement call to make late.
4. **Nothing that could read as an official symbol** — no flag, emblem, ministry mark, or
   arrangement that implies endorsement.
5. **Every motif comes from a reference image**, credited in the production file. Invented
   "Arabian-style" pattern is exactly the cliché the brief bans.
6. **Line weight consistent within a sheet**, varying only to separate foreground from background.
7. **Test every sheet at 100% print size in greyscale** before approval. Detail that reads on a
   screen closes up on paper.

## The prototype

`prototype/build_prototype.py` renders this specification as a real A4 pad, two ways:

- **`prototype-template.pdf`** — the production layout. Every sheet's typography, prompts,
  difficulty icon, numbering and 12 mm safe margin, finalised, with the illustration area empty
  and its art direction printed inside it. This is what the illustrator draws into and what the
  printer quotes against.
- **`prototype-dummy.pdf`** — the same pages with placeholder geometric line art, so a physical
  pad can be printed for the **paper and format tests**: marker bleed-through, tear-out, crayon
  behaviour, trim and drop test.

**What the dummy does not test:** subject appeal, the difficulty of the real line art, or
whether children engage with the actual drawings. The placeholder art is deliberately generic
geometry — inventing "Arabian-style" motifs would break rule 5 above, and fake Al-Qatt or Sadu
patterns would break it badly. Real engagement testing needs commissioned illustration; see
`illustrator-brief.md`, which is the critical-path gap in the whole plan.

```
python3 business/02-product/prototype/build_prototype.py --mode both
```
