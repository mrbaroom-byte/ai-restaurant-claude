# Illustrator commissioning brief

> **This is the critical-path gap.** Everything else in the pack can be run by one person in
> September. Twenty pieces of original line art cannot. Commission this in the first week or the
> 1–15 October print-proof window closes on you.

## The job

Twenty A4 black-line illustrations for a children's creative-discovery pad, plus a cover and a
completion certificate. Subjects, art direction and prompts are already specified — the
illustrator draws into a finished layout rather than designing one.

**Give them:** `prototype/prototype-template.pdf` (the layout, with each sheet's art direction
printed inside its own illustration area), `sheets.json` (the data), and
`20-sheet-content-plan.md` (the full rationale and the seven editorial rules).

## What "line art" means here

| | |
|---|---|
| Colour | Black line only. No greyscale fills, no tone, no hatching for shading |
| Stroke | Minimum 1.2 pt at final print size, consistent within a sheet |
| Weight variation | Only to separate foreground from background — not for texture |
| Enclosure | Shapes should close, so a child can fill them. Open contours frustrate 5-year-olds |
| Density | Matched to the sheet's difficulty rating: ● generous shapes, ●●● dense detail |
| Format | Vector, delivered as layered source plus outlined PDF/X |

## The seven editorial rules — non-negotiable

Carried from `20-sheet-content-plan.md`. Put them in the contract, not just the brief.

1. **Contemporary, not costume.** People wear what people wear now. Historic dress only where
   the sheet is explicitly historical.
2. **Children of visibly different backgrounds appear across the pad.**
3. **No faces on religious subjects; no religious sites as colouring subjects.**
4. **Nothing that could read as an official symbol** — no flag, emblem, ministry mark, or
   arrangement implying endorsement.
5. **Every motif comes from a reference image**, credited in the production file. Invented
   "Arabian-style" pattern is exactly the cliché this brand is positioned against.
6. **Consistent line weight within a sheet.**
7. **Test at 100% print size in greyscale before approval.** Detail that reads on a screen
   closes up on paper.

Rule 5 has teeth on three sheets in particular. **Sheet 12 (Al-Qatt Al-Asiri)** and **sheet 13
(Sadu)** depict living craft traditions with real, documented motif vocabularies. Those must be
drawn from genuine reference and reviewed by the cultural reviewer before anything else on those
sheets is approved. **Sheet 20's border** echoes both, so it inherits the same requirement.

## Delivery order — draw the risky ones first

Not sheet 1 to sheet 20. Order by how much a redraw would cost you.

| Batch | Sheets | Why first |
|-------|--------|-----------|
| 1 — style test | 1, 9, 16 | One easy, one pattern-heavy, one dense. Settles line weight, density and the difficulty ladder across the whole range before 17 more are drawn in the wrong style. |
| 2 — cultural review first | 12, 13, 20 | Reference-dependent. Get the reviewer's sign-off on the motifs before the finished art. |
| 3 — the rest of the places | 8, 10, 11, 18, 19 | Architecture and contemporary |
| 4 — nature | 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 | Largest batch, lowest risk |
| 5 — remaining | 14, 15, 17 | Craft and everyday life |
| 6 | Cover + certificate | Last, once the style is settled and you know which page is the hero |

**Approve batch 1 before commissioning batch 2.** Paying for three sheets to discover the style
is wrong is a good outcome; paying for twenty is not.

## Feed the play-test findings back in

The child sessions (Sept 7–18) produce three things the illustrator needs, so schedule batches
3–6 after them:

- Which sheets children picked **first** → the cover and hero-image decision
- Whether ages 8–9 found it babyish → add detail density to the medium sheets, do **not** add
  more sheets
- Whether children **noticed** the discovery prompts → if not, the prompt's position within the
  composition has to change, and that is an illustration problem

## Contract points

- [ ] **Full IP assignment in writing**, covering every use you intend — including the future
      extensions in the brand architecture (sticker stories, activity kits, postcards, educator
      packs). Licensing the pad only, then discovering you cannot put sheet 1 on a postcard, is
      an expensive and very common mistake.
- [ ] Source files delivered, not just exports
- [ ] Reference images credited in the production file — this is your evidence under rule 5
- [ ] Revision rounds agreed per batch, in the fee
- [ ] Delivery dates tied to the calendar in `05-operations/calendar.md`
- [ ] The seven editorial rules attached and referenced
- [ ] Cultural reviewer's sign-off named as an acceptance condition on sheets 12, 13 and 20
- [ ] Confirm the illustrator's right to grant what they are granting — that any reference,
      texture or asset they use is licensed for commercial use and assignment

**Do not agree a rate from this document.** Get quotes from at least three illustrators against
this brief; children's line art at this specification varies enormously by experience and by
market. The pilot budget's SAR 3,500 "prototype refinement & samples" line does **not** cover
twenty finished illustrations — this is an additional commitment, and pricing it is the first
thing to do, because it may change the print-run maths in `03-economics/`.

## Where to look

Saudi and wider Gulf children's-book illustrators; regional illustration agencies; the
children's publishing community at Saudi book fairs. Ask every candidate for a sample of
**black line art**, not full-colour work — they are different skills, and the pad lives or dies
on the line.

## The acceptance test for each sheet

- [ ] Prints at 100% on the specified stock and reads clearly
- [ ] Shapes close; a child can fill them
- [ ] Density matches the assigned difficulty rating
- [ ] Everything colourable sits inside the 12 mm safe margin
- [ ] Nothing breaks the seven editorial rules
- [ ] Reference credited
- [ ] Cultural reviewer signed off, by sheet number
- [ ] A real child of the target age has coloured it without asking what it is
